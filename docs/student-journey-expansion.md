## Why Speaking showed “Hàng đợi đang đầy”

That message was generated by server admission limits: two outstanding assessments for one student or 100 queued/running jobs globally. It is a server queue-limit response, not the network-offline message. Slow/provider-failed jobs can expose the limit while a student continues through a reading deck. The old client also stopped polling session progress after leaving the pending item unless the complete session had pending feedback.

The fix persists a bounded SQLite/WAV waiting spool. Busy normal admission accepts the signed immutable recording once, writes a `waiting` assessment job and returns a normal `queued` attempt with `queueState=waiting`. The worker claims waiting/queued jobs in order under the existing transaction/lease. Restart and identical upload retries cannot duplicate a job or provider charge. Session polling continues while any item is queued, assessing or awaiting feedback; students can work on another item and completed scores update in the background without moving their active card.

Queued, assessing and failed attempts, including individual items in a reading deck, appear in owner-scoped History with status/error/audio access. Unscored entries show **Chưa có điểm**, never a fabricated zero. They are excluded from completed-score averages. Pre-submission answer redaction remains in place; only a whitelist of the student's own recording status is exposed. Successful completed-set aggregation remains unchanged.

The default spool limit is 1,000 outstanding assessment jobs, to bound disk/service load. At this hard limit the upload is rejected with a specific message and the browser retains the WAV for explicit retry. The same configured pronunciation provider, original cost/daily quota, maximum three explicit grading attempts, immutable audio, retention and ownership checks remain. There is no automatic paid retry or silent provider switch. Provider failures become visible in History; valid already-scored items retain their scores.
